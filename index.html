Getting started

Finding your way around

The first time you open AcreTrace, a short tour points out what each button does, and AR mode has its own the first time you open it. To see them again, go to Settings › Apps › AcreTrace and turn on Show Tour Again.

Tips at the top of the screen suggest what to do next. If you've turned them off with Don't show tips again, the Show Tips switch in the same place brings them back.

Adding points on the map

Press and hold on the map for a moment to drop a point — a quick tap won't add one. That's deliberate: a tap is far too easy to land by accident while you're walking a boundary with the phone in your hand.

You can also press the map pin button in the results panel, beside Save, to add a point exactly where you're standing, and press a + marker between two points to insert a new one on that edge.

To move a point, press on it and a handle appears beneath it. Slide your finger down onto the handle and drag: the point follows the handle's tip, so your finger never hides where it's landing. You can do that in one motion, or let go and drag the handle afterwards. A new point from a + marker moves the same way. Tap an empty part of the map to put the handle away. To type a point's exact coordinates instead, open the point list and tap the point.

The results panel

The panel at the bottom has three heights, changed with the arrows on its top edge. The tallest one shows the unit choices and a row of buttons: volume, export, notes, photo, project and the point list. If you're looking for one of those, tap the up arrow.

Saved (the folder) and New are round buttons on the right of the map, under the location arrow.

Recording a boundary with Live Trace

On a new, empty survey, tap Live Trace and walk or drive the edge of the area. AcreTrace records your path as you go and shows the area and perimeter live. Tap Done when you're back where you started; the trace ends where you're standing.

  • You can lock the screen and put the phone in your pocket; the trace keeps recording.
  • Use Pause to skip a stretch you can't get along, such as a pond or a hedge. When you resume, the straight line from where you paused to where you are becomes that edge.
  • Live Trace needs Precise Location. If it's off, iOS asks whether to allow it for the trace.

Taking out a pond or a building (cutouts)

In area mode, raise the panel to its tallest height and tap + Cutout, then press and hold inside the boundary to place the cutout's corners. The area shown is then the boundary minus the cutout; the perimeter stays the outer edge. Tap Boundary to go back to editing the outside. A cutout drawn in orange isn't wholly inside the boundary, or overlaps another cutout, so it isn't taken off. Cutouts are part of Pro.

Projects

A project groups the surveys for one job, such as every bed and path on a landscaping site, so you can see a single total. Tap Saved on the right of the map, switch to Projects, and tap New Project. To put a survey in one, use the project button on the survey itself, press and hold it in the saved list and choose Move to Project, or add it from inside the project.

From a project you can open any of its surveys, start a new GPS or AR survey that's already filed in it, and use Show on Map to see all its GPS surveys together. Making projects and Show on Map are free; the combined totals and the project PDF report are part of Pro.

GPS mode vs AR mode

Use GPS mode for anything you can walk or trace on a map — fields, paddocks, lawns, driveways, fence lines. Use AR mode for small areas where GPS simply isn't precise enough, such as a patio, a flower bed or a single room. They're separate tools rather than two settings on one tool, because they're good at different jobs.

In AR mode, aim the crosshair at a corner and press the pin button to add a point. To move one, tap its marker, aim the crosshair where it should be, and press the pin again.

AR measurements have no map position — they're distances in the space in front of you — so they can't be exported as GPX or KML. CSV export gives you the raw coordinates in metres.

My saved survey won't let me move anything

Opening a saved survey starts it locked, so reviewing an old measurement can't accidentally change it. Tap the padlock beside the survey's name, at the top of the results panel, to unlock it. Notes, photos and the volume calculator stay available either way.

Common questions

Does it work without a signal?

Yes. Measuring, saving, the volume calculator and AR mode all work with no connection at all. Two things do need one: loading map imagery for an area you haven't visited before, and searching for a place by name.

Are my surveys backed up anywhere?

No — and this is worth knowing before you rely on it. Everything is stored on your iPhone only. There is currently no account and no cloud sync, so if you lose the phone or delete the app, the saved surveys go with it. If a measurement matters, export it and keep the file somewhere safe. Sync is the next major thing being worked on.

What's free, and what's paid?

  • Free — full GPS and AR measuring, Live Trace, unlimited AR captures, 4 saved GPS surveys (imported ones count too), and grouping surveys into projects.
  • Pro — unlimited saved surveys, the volume calculator, cutouts, project totals, and PDF reports.
  • File Export — GPX, KML, CSV and shapefile export.

Both are one-time purchases, not subscriptions. Prices are shown in your own currency in the app. Editing a survey you've already saved is never blocked, even at the free limit, and AR captures don't count towards it.

I bought it, then got a new phone — how do I get it back?

Sign in with the same Apple Account and install AcreTrace. Your purchase is normally recognised automatically, with nothing to tap.

If a feature you bought still asks you to pay, tap it to open the purchase screen and choose Restore Purchases. It checks with the App Store, and may ask for your Apple Account password. If that doesn't bring it back either, get in touch.

What files can I import?

GPX, KML, KMZ and CSV. Very long tracks are simplified as they come in, so that a five-thousand-point walk log stays workable on the map — the import summary tells you when that has happened, and the shape and area are preserved. Holes in a KML shape, and cutouts in a CSV that AcreTrace exported, come back in as cutouts. Shapefiles can't be imported; if your source can also export KML, use that instead.

How accurate is it?

GPS accuracy depends on your iPhone, the sky above you and how far you are from buildings and trees — typically a few metres per point, which is fine for a field and poor for a flower bed. That's what AR mode is for. For any measurement with money or a boundary riding on it, treat AcreTrace as a good working estimate rather than a legal survey.

How do I delete my data?

Swipe left on any saved survey, or press and hold it and choose Delete. Deleting the app removes everything it stored, permanently and immediately.

Requirements

iPhone running iOS 26.5 or later. AR mode needs a device that supports ARKit. AcreTrace is an iPhone app; it runs on iPad in compatibility mode but isn't designed for that screen yet.
