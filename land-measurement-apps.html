Land measurement apps for iPhone, compared

Eight apps for measuring acreage and distance: what each is good at, what it costs, and where it falls short. Checked 14 September 2026.

Support Privacy Policy Compare apps

AcreTrace is our app, so read this knowing that. Everything said about the other apps comes from their own App Store listings or from our hands-on testing, and says which. Each has a link so you can check for yourself.

Prices are US App Store prices on 14 September 2026. They vary by country and change often.

The short version

  • The most established app for walking a field: GPS Fields Area Measure Map.
  • Cutting ponds or buildings out of an area, plus circles: FieldCalc. Measure Map Pro and AcreTrace also do cutouts.
  • Property lines and who owns the land (US): Land id. It's a property research tool more than a measuring one.
  • Paying once, with no subscription: Planimeter, Measure Map, Measure Map Pro or AcreTrace.
  • The widest range of file formats, including CAD: Measure Map Pro.
  • Working out how much topsoil or gravel an area needs, or measuring a patio with the camera: AcreTrace. None of the other listings mention volume or camera measuring.

GPS Fields Area Measure Map

Rento UAB · 12,694 ratings, 4.6 · App Store

Good at
The most-rated app in the category. You can walk or drive a boundary, and in our walking test it kept recording accurately with the phone locked. Its listing says it works fully offline, exports KML, KMZ and shapefile, and offers cloud storage with Premium. Tapping any figure shows it in every unit.
Cost
Subscription only. When we tested it in September, the app offered $14.99 a month or $69.99 a year after a 7-day trial. Photos, groups, editing tools, export and sync sit behind it. The App Store page lists several other plans.

FieldCalc – GPS Area Measure

Pravin Gondaliya · 10,242 ratings, 4.5 · App Store

Good at
Taking ponds, buildings and other areas out of a measurement, which its listing leads with. It also measures circles from a centre and radius, lets you walk or tap out a shape, and exports to 11 formats, including shapefile, DXF and GeoJSON.
Cost
Subscription. When we tested it in September, the app offered $19.99 a month or $49.99 a year. The App Store page also lists one-time purchases, but the app itself offered only the subscription.
Worth knowing
In our test, the free version kept two saved measurements of each type, and deleting one didn't free the slot. Locking the phone partway through a walk lost the position until it was unlocked.

Measure Map

Xuan Luong Do · 5,294 ratings, 4.7 · App Store

Good at
One of the best-rated apps here. It imports and exports KML, KMZ and CSV files.
Cost
Free with ads. One-time upgrades: Pro for $7.99, file export for $5.99, and ad removal for $5.99. No subscription.

Map Area Calculator – Marea

Applorium Ltd · 4,732 ratings, 4.5 · App Store

Good at
Walking a perimeter, dropping pins automatically as you go. Its listing also covers checking property lines and lot sizes, and exporting KML, PDF or a map image.
Cost
Subscription, from $7.99 a month, with yearly plans listed at $19.99 and $39.99. It also lists a $39.99 lifetime purchase.

Land id

MapRight, LLC · 4,115 ratings, 4.6 · App Store

Good at
A different job: property research. It shows parcel boundaries and ownership data across the US, and offers offline maps, GPS tracking for fieldwork, and CSV reports of property and owner data.
Cost
Subscription. Its listing starts at $84 a year and goes up to $799.99 a year for Pro Unlimited.
Worth knowing
If you need to know where a property line is or who owns the land next door, this is the tool. For measuring your own field or yard, it's more than you need.

Planimeter – Measure Land Area

Core Signals LLC · 2,213 ratings, 4.7 · App Store

Good at
On the App Store since 2011, with the highest average rating on this page. You pay once to own it.
Cost
$7.99 upfront. An optional Pro subscription adds more satellite map sources.
Worth knowing
Its listing is short and doesn't mention walking a boundary, cutouts or file export, so check it does what you need.

Measure Map Pro

Blue Blink One, SL · 829 ratings, 4.6 · App Store

Good at
The widest file support here. It exports KML, KMZ, CSV, DXF for AutoCAD, shapefile, GPX, PDF and images, and imports KML, KMZ and GPX. It can also cut holes out of a shape, and it supports iPad.
Cost
Free, with one-time upgrades: $4.99 for the Regular version, $30.99 for Pro, and $28.99 for an extra maps package.

AcreTrace (ours)

John McIntosh · New, no ratings yet · Coming to the App Store

Good at
Working out how much material an area needs: enter a depth and it gives the volume, with an allowance for settling and waste. For patios and beds too small for GPS, the camera measures in AR, free. You can also walk a boundary with the screen locked, cut ponds or buildings out of an area, group surveys into projects with combined totals, and produce PDF reports. It imports GPX, KML, KMZ and CSV. No account needed.
Cost
Free for AR and for up to five saved GPS surveys. Pro is a one-time $9.99 for unlimited saves, volume, cutouts, project totals and PDF reports. File Export is a one-time $4.99 for GPX, KML, CSV and shapefile. No subscription.
Where it falls short
It's new, so there are no reviews to go on yet. There's no cloud sync or backup, so a lost phone means lost surveys. It has no property lines. Measuring works without a signal, but map imagery may not load. It's iPhone only, and in English only.

Are they accurate?

About as accurate as each other. In September we walked the same field boundary with AcreTrace, FieldCalc and GPS Fields. The three areas came out between 19,630 and 19,721 m², within half a percent of each other. Along a 750 m boundary, that's like walking the line about 11 cm further out, far less than a phone's GPS can be off by. The phone sets the accuracy far more than the app does. For anything with money or a legal boundary riding on it, use a survey, not a phone.
